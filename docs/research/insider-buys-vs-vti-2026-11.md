## Bottom line

**In the stocks Trader can buy, following insiders' open-market purchases has not beaten VTI, and the live strategy's version of it has lost to VTI outside 2020.**

1. **No edge overall.** 11,294 tradable events trailed VTI by 0.4% over 60 trading days and by 1.6% over 120. 46% beat VTI.
2. **The live strategy's pattern** (2+ officers or directors buying after a 20%+ drop) shows +1.5% over 60 days across all years. That is **2020's crash rebound** (301 events at +12.4%). Without 2020 it lost to VTI: **−2.1% ± 0.9% over 60 days and −7.2% over 120**. The year-to-year swings are large (2021 −16.3%, 2025 −5.6%).
3. **Holding longer made it worse.** 120-day results are below 60-day results in almost every bucket and year, so any rebound after insider buying was short-lived.
4. **Outcomes are lottery-like.** About one in five live-pattern events beat VTI by more than 20 points over 60 days, and about one in five trailed it by more than 20. Single positions swing far more than the averages suggest.
5. **The real picture is probably worse.** 4,802 events had no Alpaca price history (delisted, merged or renamed companies). Those are more often failures, so leaving them out flatters every figure here.

## What it means for Trader

- **An insider buy is not an edge by itself in liquid stocks.** Risk only allows stocks at $5 or more that trade $10M or more a day, and 27,221 of the 43,609 events failed that filter. Insider buying tends to be concentrated in small, thinly traded companies, which may be where any edge lives, and Trader can't buy those.
- **This doesn't test Claude's judgment on top of the signal.** The routine picks a handful of names from many and adds fundamental research, and that layer could still add value. But the base rate it starts from is negative, so an insider buy should be one input to a thesis, not the thesis.
- **For the routine prompt (B5), to decide with Scott:**
  - Treat insider buying as weak supporting evidence rather than a primary signal.
  - Don't let one kind of signal fill the book. Today every live thesis is an insider buy.
  - Be wary of holding an insider-dip thesis past about three months without fresh evidence.

## Caveats

- **Survivorship:** described above. Read every figure as a best case.
- **Entry:** the first close after the filing date, not a fill. Spreads, taxes and position sizing aren't modelled.
- **Overlap:** events cluster in time (2020 especially), so they aren't independent. The year-by-year tables are the check, and they show the result isn't stable.
- **Fixed buckets:** the breakdowns were set before the run (plus routine vs opportunistic, added after a spot check found scheduled director buys). Nothing was searched for a pattern that happens to work.
- **Routine vs opportunistic** barely matters here: only 196 events from 2020 on were all-routine.

Routine buyers (Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012) bought the same issuer in the same calendar month in each of the two prior years; the rest are opportunistic. Classifying needs two years of history, so those breakdowns start in 2020.

Excess return = the stock's return minus VTI's over the same trading days, from the first close after the last filing. Means are of per-event excess returns; SE is the standard error of the mean.
