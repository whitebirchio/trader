# Earnings reactions vs VTI, 2018–2026

> Backtest for the #15 rethink ([plan](../../tasks/done/earnings-backtest-plan.md)), run 2026-11-08: earnings releases (8-K item 2.02) from SEC's per-company filing index, Alpaca's adjusted daily bars, and the insider events from [#29](insider-buys-vs-vti-2026-11.md). The summary is hand-written from the tables below, which `npm run research:earnings -w @trader/core -- --report ../../docs/research/earnings-reactions-vs-vti-2026-11.md` regenerates.

## Bottom line

**In the stocks Trader can buy, the market's reaction to earnings, up or down, hasn't been followed by beating VTI. Big earnings-day drops kept losing afterwards.**

1. **The baseline is negative.** Across 54,613 tradable releases, the stock trailed VTI by 0.8% over the next 60 trading days (median −1.8%, 45% beat VTI). Most individual stocks lag a broad index over most windows, so every bucket below should be read against that baseline, not against zero.
2. **No buyable drift after a beat.** Stocks up 10%+ on the reaction day did −0.7% over 60 days (−1.7% without 2020), no better than the baseline. The classic post-earnings drift doesn't show up in liquid stocks over this period.
3. **Buying an earnings-day crash lost.** Stocks down 10%+ did −1.7% over 60 days, and **−2.7% (60 days) and −5.3% (120 days) without 2020**, worse than the baseline. Several live theses (LKQ, TTMI, LSTR, INTC) were bought after exactly this kind of drop.
4. **Insider buying before the release helps a little, relatively, but not enough.** With an officer or director buy in the prior 90 days, −0.3% vs −0.9% without (about two standard errors apart). Among drops of 5% or more, +0.2% vs −1.3%. That's consistent with insider buying as weak supporting evidence, and not a reason to buy.
5. **The years swing hard and 2020 flatters every average,** as in #29. In 2021, 2023 and 2024 the big-move buckets lost 2–11 points against VTI.

## What it means for Trader

- Neither signal family tested so far (insider buying, earnings reactions) has beaten VTI in liquid stocks on its own. Together with the negative single-stock baseline, that says the account's default posture (5–8 single-stock theses holding most of the money) has started each bet from behind.
- **"Buy after the earnings crash, because insiders bought" has been a losing pattern,** both alone and here. It's what the live book mostly holds.
- **For the routine (to decide with Scott):**
  - Stop treating earnings beats as a lead signal.
  - Avoid buying into a fresh earnings-day crash without a reason the market has demonstrably missed.
  - Hold the bar for a thesis higher (several independent signals, a clear mispricing), with VTI as the honest default when that bar isn't met.
- **#15 (a discovery screen):** neither signal earns a screen built around it. Close #15 with this reason, or keep it only as a convenience for research, not as a source of edge.

## Caveats

- **Survivorship is worse than in #29:** SEC's ticker list holds only companies trading today. Firms that delisted or merged since 2018 are missing, so every figure here is likely too generous.
- **The reaction stands in for the surprise:** there are no consensus estimates (they aren't free). The reaction includes guidance, which arguably makes it a better measure.
- **Item 2.02 isn't only quarterly results:** pre-announcements and other results releases count too (a company pre-announcing a weak quarter, for example). A second release on the same reaction day is dropped.
- **Entry** is the close of the session after the reaction day, not a fill. Spreads and taxes aren't modelled.
- **Overlap:** releases cluster in earnings seasons, so they aren't independent. The year-by-year tables are the check.

**Data:** 6,172 companies from SEC's current ticker list with Alpaca bars; 110,673 earnings releases (8-K item 2.02) filed 2018-01-01 to 2026-06-30.

**Scored:** 54,613 releases. **Left out:** 2,049 without the bars around the reaction day, 53,502 that failed risk's price/liquidity filters at entry, 0 with no bar after the reaction day, 0 with no bars, 0 without a VTI close. Companies that no longer trade aren't in SEC's current list at all (the survivorship gap).

Reaction = the stock's return minus VTI's on the first session that could trade on the release (the same day if accepted before 9:30 ET, else the next). Entry is the close of the session after the reaction day. Excess return = the stock's return minus VTI's from entry over the same trading days. Means are of per-release excess returns; SE is the standard error of the mean.
