# Quality and value vs VTI, 2019–2026

> Backtest for [#49](https://github.com/whitebirchio/trader/issues/49), run 2026-11-08 from SEC's XBRL frames and Alpaca's daily bars ([plan](../../tasks/done/momentum-quality-plan.md)). Decided on the 2019–2023 April cohorts, with 2024–2025 held out under a promotion rule fixed before any results (the 250-trading-day excess, after a 0.2% round trip). The summary is hand-written from the tables below, which `npm run research:quality -w @trader/core -- --report ../../docs/research/quality-value-vs-vti-2026-11.md` regenerates.

## Bottom line

**No quality or value metric passes the promotion rule. Profitability spreads were real in-sample, but the top deciles didn't beat VTI, and the held-out 2025 cohort reversed sharply.**

1. **Nothing passes.** Over the following year, the top deciles in-sample were −2.3% vs VTI (gross profitability), +5.6% (earnings yield), +1.0% (book-to-market) and +6.9% (quality plus value), each with standard errors of 12–17 points over just five cohorts. Held out, all four top deciles trailed VTI (−2.6% to −18.1%).
2. **Profitability sorted stocks in-sample.** Top minus bottom for gross profitability was positive in **every** in-sample cohort (+10.5% ± 2.5% a year), and quality plus value +19.7% ± 6.4%. That's consistent with published work, and like momentum, most of it came from the bottom decile doing badly.
3. **The held-out 2025 cohort reversed.** From April 2025 to April 2026, low-profitability and low-quality stocks rallied (bottom deciles +23% to +39% vs VTI), flipping every spread. With only two held-out cohorts, one regime can dominate. That's exactly why the rule needed the held-out years to agree before anything is promoted.
4. **A common thread across all four studies:** most buckets trail VTI by double digits in years like 2019 and 2021–2023. That's mostly not about the signals. It's an **equal-weighted universe of mostly small and mid caps against a cap-weighted VTI** in years when mega-caps led. Any single-stock thesis starts from that gap.

## What it means for Trader

- **Don't add a quality or value lead signal** on this evidence.
- **Low profitability is a mild warning, like weak momentum:** the bottom profitability decile lagged in every in-sample cohort and in 2024. It didn't in 2025's junk rally, though, so it's context, not a rule.
- **Size the single-stock gap honestly.** Across insider buys, earnings reactions, momentum and quality, the typical tradable stock lagged VTI. A thesis needs a reason to beat that baseline, not just the absence of a red flag.

## Caveats

- **Survivorship:** companies are matched to SEC's *current* ticker list, so firms that delisted since 2018 are missing (likely flattering the bottom deciles and every bucket against VTI).
- **Frames carry no filing date.** Figures count only if their period ended 75+ days before formation. Values come from the latest filing that reports them, usually an identical comparative column; a genuine restatement can leak a revised figure (LKQ's 2022 net income shows $1,150M here vs $1,149M in the original 10-K).
- **A data-bug fix, made before reading the results:** a first version also required the accession's filing year to be no later than the formation year. It removed 75–86% of real figures (later 10-Ks' comparative columns), leaving 224–306 companies per cohort. It was dropped, and only the corrected run is reported.
- **Few cohorts:** 5 in-sample and 2 held-out, with stocks in a cohort moving together. The standard errors are wide.

**Data:** SEC XBRL frames for fiscal data years 2018–2025, scored at the last session of each April (2019-04-30, 2020-04-30, 2021-04-30, 2022-04-29, 2023-04-28, 2024-04-30, 2025-04-30, 2026-04-30). Companies are matched to SEC's *current* ticker list (the survivorship gap). Market value uses Alpaca's unadjusted April close. **In-sample:** the 2019–2023 cohorts. **Held out:** 2024 on (2026's year isn't complete).

A figure counts for a formation only if its period ended at least 75 days before (so a fiscal year ending after formation is left out). Frames take each figure from the latest filing that reports it, usually a later 10-K's comparative column with the same value; a genuine restatement can leak a revised figure. (A rule requiring the accession's filing year to be no later than the formation year was tried first and dropped before reading any results: it removed 75–86% of real figures, the comparatives, leaving 1–300 companies per cohort.) Each cohort ranks the tradable companies (risk's filters at the next close) into deciles (10 = highest metric). A decile's result is the mean excess over its companies, and the statistics run over the **cohorts**, so there are only 5 in-sample and 2–3 held-out observations: read the standard errors with that in mind.

**Promotion rule (fixed before any results):** the top decile's mean 250-day excess must beat VTI in-sample, and in the held-out 2024–2025 cohorts after a 0.2% round-trip cost.
